# The Lantern Sea

## The promise

A black shape crosses Port Lumen's moonlit mouth. Three Hearthlight merchants are minutes from the lamps, but the chart holds a fading report, `B3 · wake toward C3 · 01:42`. From a destroyer bridge, the player turns toward the sound and chooses whether the freighters hold course or scatter through Rook Bay's island channel. A plume lands wide. A new wake moves from B3 to C3. Now the player can use the destroyer's search pulse on C3, or race home while the contact remains uncertain. Dawn and the harbour lamps tell the truth. Every ship that reaches them is remembered by name.

**The Lantern Sea** is a fictional, WWII-inspired island-sea campaign about bringing a mixed flotilla home. The player commands two to five warships through bridge and periscope views, then opens a live chart for orders and incomplete reports. NPC merchants and the tender travel with the force and cannot be directly controlled. Combat protects passage, intelligence, repair parts, and people waiting at the port.

Blindfire supplies the desired uncertainty. Its **Cold-to-Scorching** landing feedback gives a player a reason to adjust a second shot. Lantern Sea carries that rhythm into naval combat. After a shot, a lookout might see a plume or smoke. A scout might report a fresh wake. Sometimes nobody sees where it landed. Confidence ages on the chart. Broadside supplies visible flooding, smoke, and local failure as readable consequences.

## The playable loop

Each sortie asks the player to **spot, decide, position, commit, and recover**.

1. **Spot** through a ship view, a friendly observer, or an incoming wire report.
2. **Decide** whether the next promise is convoy safety, interception, rescue, or fuel for the next branch.
3. **Position** the active ship directly and give each ally one short order.
4. **Commit** an arcade action to a chart sector or visual target, then read what the sea actually shows.
5. **Recover** at sea or in harbour, choosing what can be saved before the campaign advances.

On the bridge, time is real. Opening the chart slows it to a readable crawl. Solo play has a dedicated pause. A short sortie runs 8–12 minutes, with a final showdown allowed 18. `W/S` set speed, `A/D` steer, mouse aims, left click commits, `M` opens the chart, `Tab` changes ship, and `1–4` choose orders. Touch has a steering stick, aim drag, Commit, Chart, and Command buttons. Captions, icons, and readable silhouettes make the full game playable muted.

## A fight with a second thought

The chart is a 4×4 grid, sectors `A1` through `D4`. A player can mark a sector from a live report and commit an action there, or use the ship view when a target is visible. Hidden ships are never placed as exact icons. A **glimpse** gives direction and time, a **wake** gives movement, a **wire report** suggests class, and a **sound mark** gives rough underwater location. Each has one to three confidence pips, then turns into a question mark as it ages. Both commanders use these same rules.

Each warship earns an immediate, satisfying action.

| Hull | Distinct arcade action and purpose | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Submarine | Spend one limited torpedo token from a periscope line, then manage battery while slipping away. | Low battery leaves it slow and exposed when surfacing. |
| Destroyer | Send a search pulse or depth-charge pattern into a marked sector to force a wake, reveal, or retreat. | It leaves the merchants and can chase a decoy. |
| Cruiser | Launch a scout flare or smoke screen across one sector to create a lane. | The flare exposes it and repairs cost dearly. |
| Battleship | Fire a loud, slow-recharging salvo with a visible footprint that can deny a channel. | It consumes time and fuel while drawing pressure toward its guard. |
| Merchant or tender | Deliver cargo, survivors, or one at-sea component repair. | It needs escorts and creates painful tradeoffs. |

In Rook Bay, the first destroyer pattern lands wide in C3. It produces a fresh wake into C4 and a lookout's wire report, “large silhouette, eastbound.” The player may let *Moth* spend its torpedo token on the new mark, use *Harrow*'s flare to reveal it, or order the destroyer back to screen the tender. The opposing commander receives and loses information in the same way.

Allies keep formation, avoid obvious collisions, reload, and protect themselves automatically. The player makes the consequential calls. **Screen the convoy** widens destroyer patrols. **Hold B2** keeps a cruiser ready. **Shadow C4** asks a submarine to report without attacking. **Run for Lumen** favors merchant schedule. The chart previews route and a plain fuel, exposure, or time cost. The player can take direct control of any warship at once.

Damage may disable steering, speed, radio, sensors, or an action. Flooding grows, fires spread, and fatigue slows response. At a lull, choose patch steering, fight fire, tow a cripple, or keep watch. One priority leads. A tender restores one disabled component while becoming a slow target. At Port Lumen, the cruiser can take the single repair berth while a substitute sails its next leg. A ship that limps in keeps its scar through recovery.

## A campaign of lit ports

Port Lumen is the home port and campaign hub. The returning ships appear at its docks, where the player assigns the repair berth and chooses the next leg. Rook Bay is a contested island approach near the Morrow Isles, with narrow channels, ambush sightlines, and no safe repair facility.

A complete campaign has 12 authored missions, with roughly eight in one run.

- **Hearth Straits** teaches chart reading and escort choice among shoals and civilian traffic.
- **Morrow Isles** is a night archipelago where lamps guide one route and expose another.
- **Whitecap Reach** is an icy outer sea where visibility and fuel determine whether damaged ships can make the final run.

Losses change one named later rule. If *Celandine* grounds outside Rook Bay, **The Celandine Recovery** replaces the next standard resupply mission. Its tender begins at half repair stock and its chart begins with a reliable `B2 · survivor flare` report. Recovering its cargo restores the tender's full repair action for the mission after that. A merchant loss therefore changes the next decision without compounding into a campaign collapse.

In **The Narrow Bell**, the player begins in destroyer *Wren* on a night screen, shifts to submarine *Moth* for a periscope approach after its relay reports pursuit, then returns to *Wren* for the convoy's last minute. Earlier surface orders remain live during the perspective change.

### Sample mission — The lamps at Rook Bay

Three NPC Hearthlight merchants and the tender enter Rook Bay under low cloud. The player brings the destroyer *Wren*, cruiser *Harrow*, and submarine *Moth*. *Harrow* has a damaged radio. A fading `C3 · wake east` report comes from *Moth* behind the islands.

The player sends *Wren* to Shadow C3, keeps *Harrow* on a protective line, and guides *Moth* by periscope toward C4. The first destroyer pattern produces the wake and wire report described above. The player chooses *Harrow*'s flare instead of spending *Moth*'s torpedo. The raider reveals itself long enough to damage *Celandine* and the tender's steering, while *Wren* keeps it outside the channel. Two merchants and the tender reach Port Lumen. *Celandine* grounds outside the lamps.

That is a partial victory with a specific next leg, **The Celandine Recovery**. The port receives supplies and the tender survives. The player decides whether its single berth repairs *Harrow*'s radio or the tender's steering for the recovery mission.

The campaign ends at **Breakwater Dawn**, a Whitecap Reach fleet showdown. To win, escort at least two surviving Hearthlight merchants across D4 and keep the enemy flagship outside the exit during the final chart beat. A battleship salvo, submarine contact, cruiser screen, or destroyer pursuit can create that window. If too few merchants survive or the flagship takes the exit, the campaign moves into **Empty Quay**, a final sortie to bring Port Lumen’s crews ashore on a nearby island. The ending records who arrived and who was saved.

## Presentation and production shape

On the parchment chart, large sector letters and distinct hull profiles stay legible at phone size. The ship view uses painted water, smoke, lamps, wakes, and chunky component damage. Engines, rain, bells, and distant impacts support captions and visual cues. The fictional setting needs no historical factions, insignia, or real operations.

The HTML rollout has three slices.

1. **Chart encounter** has a convoy, destroyer, sectors, aging tokens, one commit action, and full or partial results. Acceptance is a five-minute repeatable encounter where every token has an explainable origin and fade.
2. **Flotilla encounter** adds all five hull roles, switching, component damage, repair priority, and quick battle. Acceptance is five short scenarios where each hull changes the winning plan and orders stay clear without micromanagement.
3. **Lantern campaign** adds regions, branches, continuity, recovery, Breakwater Dawn, local saves after every completed leg, and browser resume from the campaign chart. Acceptance is two complete routes with distinct recovery consequences.

The chart-only first slice uses a lightweight 2D canvas renderer. Ship scenes stay modest for the browser budget. Offline single-player and quick battle are the baseline. Two-player co-command is a later stretch. Cut multiplayer, extra campaign variation, special mechanics, then dynamic weather. Keep the convoy, sector intelligence, direct control, five silhouettes, and meaningful damage.
